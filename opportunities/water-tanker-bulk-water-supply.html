2. Water Tanker / Bulk Water Supply (housing societies, construction, commercial)

Verdict: WEAK-MEDIUM (do not pursue as fresh entry) · Sector: Water / Logistics · Model: B2B/B2C Service · Country: India (Mumbai/Bangalore)
Entry capital: N/A - rejected · EBITDA timeline: N/A - rejected · Margins: N/A

The idea: Own/operate a small fleet of water tankers supplying bulk water to housing societies, construction sites, and industrial/commercial buildings.

💰 Unit economics / positive-EBITDA-ASAP logic

  • Even at regulated caps: Rs 700-1,500 per 6,000-12,000L trip; peak-crisis prices reached Rs 2,200-2,500/12,000L in Bengaluru 2024-25.
  • Pune market benchmark: ~Rs 23 cr/day, 3,500 tankers, 22,000-30,000 trips/day, 20% of population dependent.
  • BUT margins are being squeezed by municipal supply expansion — the long-run thesis is negative for private entrants.

⚠️ Watch-outs / next dig cycle

  • This is the canonical 'pattern-based intuition looks good, live data says otherwise' example — exactly why the hunter pulls fresh 2026/27 sources instead of stale training logic.
  • Kept in the database as a documented negative case to show the reasoning, not as a recommendation.

❌ Why it was rejected

  • Demand is real but the moat is ERODING on live 2026 data: BWSSB's Sanchari Cauvery scheme (launched May 2025) delivered 30,000+ tankers / Rs 5+ crore by April 2026 and has already cut private tanker demand ~60% in some pockets.
  • Heavy regulation: Karnataka fixed 12,000L at Rs 1,200; forced registration with BBMP; GPS tracking mandates; 200+ private tankers taken over by the state.
  • Documented union/mafia behavior (blocking competitors, coercion, E.coli water scandals) makes fresh entry structurally contested and reputationally dangerous.
  • Government is effectively the competitor: BWSSB is adding municipal supply (Cauvery V Stage augmenting to 400-450 MLD; 3 lakh connections targeted by 2028).
