1. Commercial Kitchen Exhaust (Hood) Cleaning Service

Verdict: STRONG · Sector: Facility Services · Model: B2B Service · Country: India (Mumbai/Bangalore)
Entry capital: 5-15 L · EBITDA timeline: Fast (1-3 months) · Margins: 25-40% net (global comps up to 55%)

The idea: Deep-clean grease from commercial kitchen exhaust hoods, ducts and fans for restaurants, hotels, cafeterias, cloud kitchens. Compliance-mandated recurring service.

✅ Why it clears the filter (the data, not vibes)

  • Mandatory + recurring demand: fire-safety code means every commercial kitchen MUST have exhaust cleaned on a fixed schedule (quarterly for high-volume). Customer has no way out and cannot DIY — strongest possible cash-flow signal.
  • Net margins 25-40% per job; a single-truck operation with certified techs can do ~USD 600k/yr revenue; two trucks = seven figures.
  • Low capex ~Rs 5-15 L — fits 1 Cr entry with huge headroom. 40-60 accounts within a 15-mile radius is the profitable route-density target.
  • India providers already proven: Mumbai (OXY, Accordible), Bangalore (deepcleaning.in, EPSCO) — market is real, not theoretical.

💰 Unit economics / positive-EBITDA-ASAP logic

  • Per-job pricing: Rs 3,000-15,000+ for a single hood; mid-size restaurants Rs 8,000-15,000; large hotels Rs 15,000-30,000. Global comps: USD 450-650/system, emergency cleans USD 800+.
  • Recurring contracts (MSAs) lock in recurring revenue: a 10-hood quarterly account ~USD 2,200/visit at ~55% margin before overhead.
  • A real 60-day practitioner case study built 30 accounts / USD 60k/yr; a hospital-network example scaled to USD 850k/yr by year 2.

⚠️ Watch-outs / next dig cycle

  • Labour + night work is the core constraint: dirty, after-hours (11pm-4am), heavy PPE. Hard to hire reliable night staff.
  • CERTIFICATION GAP: 67% of these businesses fail between months 12-24, often from underpricing by 40-60%, scattered routes, and buying worthless online certifications that fire marshals/insurers don't recognise. Use approved in-person cert bodies.
  • India fire-code enforcement is less uniformly strict than the US — the 'mandatory' moat is weaker for small independents; pitch the compliance/insurance angle to larger chains where it bites.
  • Insurance: general liability does NOT cover grease-fire claims that happen after you leave. Get specialised cover vetted by a commercial-liability attorney.
